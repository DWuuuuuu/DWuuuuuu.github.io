About me

I came to AI through probability. During my PhD at Penn, I studied random geometry and earned a master’s in statistics at Wharton. My work in causal inference asks how much we can learn from comparisons in observational data. One example is our study of high school football participation and cognitive functioning later in life. Read the preprint entry

At Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, I developed GestaltMML to bring facial images and clinical text into the same rare-disease model. At Mina Analytics, I used building and lease data to estimate how the value of green certification varies from one property to another.

At Epigram, I build quantitative interview problems with multiple verified solutions. They give me a way to examine how a reasoning model reaches an answer. I’m interested in carrying that attention to measurement into clinical AI and into research on how AI changes economic decisions.
